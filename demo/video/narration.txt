Before an AI camera fines anyone, EventProof puts the rule, and every ticket, on trial. Cities like Takoma Park, Maryland, already issue forty-dollar tickets from AI stop-sign cameras. But nobody checks the rule the camera runs.
This is the actual Maryland law: shall stop at a clearly marked stop line. There is no number in it. A camera needs two: how long to stand still, and how slow counts as stopped. Muse Spark compiled the statute and had to assume both. Drag them, and the same law, on the same thirty real cars, issues anywhere from zero to seventeen tickets.
Here is how a rule is built. Muse Spark turns a sentence into a typed rule, never code, and lists every assumption it made. SAM 3.1 tracks every car in real drone footage, and a deterministic engine judges each one, with the exact frames. This van crawls through the line without ever stopping.
Now every ticket goes on trial. This car drove through the line without stopping, and the ticket is correct. Muse Spark, acting as the driver's lawyer, picks its arguments from a closed menu, and the engine checks each one. One wins: the rule never says how slow counts as stopped. Double that number, and the ticket disappears.
Every real ticket gets the same trial: the line moved a few pixels, another point of the car measured, thresholds moved, another meaning of stop. Only four of twenty hold up. So the policy is simple: issue only what survives, and send the rest to a human, with the reason. And a city learns it needs one hundred eighty-eight labelled tickets before it can promise fewer than two percent wrong ones.
Anyone can type a new rule on a real clip. Remain behind the stop line for two seconds. Muse Spark, and a second Meta model, Muse Wonder, both read remain as simply being there, so cars that never stopped pass. Two models agreeing is not proof. The certificate checks the rule against frozen labels, and refuses it.
Every test is pre-registered before any model call, and every number is recomputed in CI. On thirty labelled real cars, the rule Muse Spark compiled gets twenty-seven right, with no wrong verdicts. A repair that passed every synthetic test gets sixteen. Muse Spark alone, watching the video, counts the cars right in only eleven of twenty-five runs.
