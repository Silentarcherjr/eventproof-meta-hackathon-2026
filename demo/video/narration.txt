AI can now build a video checker from one sentence. EventProof is AI-generated vision software that doesn't just claim to work: it searches for evidence that it might be wrong.
Cities have started writing stop-sign tickets from AI cameras. Takoma Park, Maryland, fines drivers forty dollars per citation. But what counts as a full stop is a rule someone wrote, and experts say these systems are rarely validated independently.
Here is real drone footage of a crossing. The rule: every car must wait one and a half seconds before the stop line. Muse Spark turns it into a typed rule, never code, and writes down how it read it: wait means standing still. SAM 3.1 tracks every car, and a deterministic engine judges each one. This van crawls through without stopping: a violation, with the exact frames.
Now a reviewer types a slightly different rule on the same clip. Muse Spark reads remain as simply being there, and so does a second Meta model, Muse Wonder. Every car passes, including the two that never stopped. Two models agreeing is not proof.
So EventProof issues a certificate instead of trusting them. It attacks the rule with synthetic scenes, and it checks it against this clip's hand labels, frozen before any model ran. Verdict: not certified, three of five, the two cars that never stopped would have been let go.
On a warehouse version of the same rule, Challenge Mode finds 44 failures in 150 scenes and shrinks one to a minimal pair. Muse Spark proposes bounded repairs, and an independent grader keeps only those with zero regressions on 200 hidden scenes: 144 correct becomes 164.
But passing synthetic tests is not enough. On real footage from two cities, that repair calls crawling cars still. Across 30 labelled cars, Muse Spark's own rule gets 27 right with no wrong verdicts; the repair gets 16. And Muse Spark judging the video alone miscounts the cars in 14 of 25 runs.
Build it, break it, repair it, and certify it on real video, before a camera acts on it. Every number here is recomputed in CI from committed evidence.
